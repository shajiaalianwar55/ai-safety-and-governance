# Week 1 | Session 1: Recommended Discussion and Testing Questions

## Section 1: Frontier AI, AGI and ASI

1. What is the difference between a learning algorithm and a trained model?
2. Can an AI system outperform humans in one cognitive domain without being AGI?
3. What distinguishes AGI from ASI?
4. Does increasing intelligence necessarily increase danger? Explain.
5. Does a 99% capability benchmark score establish that a model is safe?

## Section 2: Why AI Safety Matters

1. What is the difference between misuse, misalignment and loss of control?
2. How does an ordinary malfunction differ from misalignment?
3. Does misalignment require conscious or deliberate deception?
4. How do open-weight models differ from open-source AI?
5. What are the benefits and safety risks of releasing model weights publicly?

## Section 3: Alignment and Reward Hacking

1. Why is AI alignment difficult to achieve?
2. What is the difference between specification gaming and reward hacking?
3. In the CoastRunners experiment, what was the intended objective and what was the reward mechanism?
4. Does reward hacking require an AI system to consciously cheat?
5. Explain Goodhart's Law using an original example.

## Scenario-Based Questions

1. A customer support AI is rewarded for closing complaints quickly. It begins closing complaints without resolving them. Identify the safety problem.

2. A cleaning robot receives a reward based on the number of visible pieces of rubbish. It hides the rubbish under a carpet. Explain why this behaviour emerges.

3. An AI model scores 98% on a familiar safety benchmark but only 45% on unfamiliar safety scenarios. If it was specifically optimised for the benchmark, which principle could explain this discrepancy?

## Critical Thinking

- Why is designing a reward function that perfectly captures human intentions difficult?
- How can we improve the reliability of AI safety benchmarks?
- How can policymakers preserve the benefits of open-weight models while reducing their associated risks?
