# Week 1 | Session 1: Recommended Readings

## Essential Readings

### 1. International AI Safety Report 2026: Executive Summary

**Organisation:** International AI Safety Report  
**Published:** 2026

Covers general-purpose AI capabilities, malicious use, malfunctions, systemic risks and approaches to risk management.

[Read here](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/2026-report-executive-summary)

### 2. Specification Gaming: The Flip Side of AI Ingenuity

**Organisation:** Google DeepMind  
**Published:** 2020

Explains specification gaming through real examples of AI systems exploiting imperfectly defined objectives.

[Read here](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/)

### 3. Faulty Reward Functions in the Wild

**Organisation:** OpenAI  
**Published:** 2016

Introduces reward misspecification through the CoastRunners reinforcement learning experiment discussed during the session.

[Read here](https://openai.com/index/faulty-reward-functions/)

## Further Readings

### 4. Levels of AGI for Operationalizing Progress on the Path to AGI

**Organisation:** Google DeepMind  
**Published:** 2023 (initial preprint)

Proposes a framework for classifying AGI capabilities based on performance and generality, while considering autonomy and associated risks.

[Read here](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02462)

### 5. Frontier AI: Capabilities and Risks

**Organisation:** UK Government  
**Published:** 2023

A discussion paper examining frontier AI capabilities, emerging risks and potential safety challenges.

[Read here](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/frontier-ai-capabilities-and-risks-discussion-paper/frontier-ai-capabilities-and-risks-discussion-paper)

### 6. Concrete Problems in AI Safety

**Published:** 2016

A foundational research paper covering five technical AI safety problems: negative side effects, reward hacking, scalable supervision, safe exploration and distributional shift.

[Read here](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06565)

### 7. The Open Source AI Definition 1.0

**Organisation:** Open Source Initiative  
**Published:** 2024

Defines the freedoms and technical requirements associated with open-source AI, helping distinguish open-source systems from models that merely release their weights.

[Read here](https://opensource.org/ai/open-source-ai-definition)

### 8. Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law

**Authors:** David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant  
**Published:** 2018 (initial preprint)

Explores four mechanisms through which optimising a proxy measure can undermine its usefulness as a measure of the intended objective.

[Read here](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)
